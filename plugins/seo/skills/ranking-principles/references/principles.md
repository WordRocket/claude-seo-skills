# The 12 ranking principles, with sources (checked Nov 2026)

Each: the principle · why it works (source) · how the writer applies it.

Format: **Principle** (one plain sentence) · *Why it works* (source) · *How the writer applies it*.

### 1. Write for one reader with one problem, and decide what "done" looks like for them.
- *Why:* Google asks whether you have "an existing or intended audience" and whether the reader leaves having "learned enough about a topic to help achieve their goal" (people-first page). Raters judge results by user intent, Know / Do / Website / Visit-in-person (QRG 12.7). A reader who has to "search again" is a warning sign (people-first page).
- *How:* Before writing, write one line: who is searching, the words they'd type, what they're trying to get done (intent type), and what they'll do after reading. Know Simple → a 1–2 sentence answer. Do → steps. Visit-in-person → hours, location, proof. Cut anything that serves a different reader.

### 2. Let the title promise it and the first lines deliver it (answer first).
- *Why:* QRG 5.2.2: "place the most helpful and essential MC near the top of the page." Filler on top = Low. Google asks if the title is "a descriptive, helpful summary" that avoids "exaggerating" (people-first page); Highest pages "have titles that summarize the page" (QRG 8.0). In ChatGPT, 44.2% of citations come from the first 30% of a page (Indig/Gauge, correlational). Search Essentials: put the words people use "in prominent locations on the page, such as the title and main heading."
- *How:* Title = the reader's words + the specific promise. The first 2–3 sentences answer the question outright, then the page explains, proves and handles the exceptions. No warm-up intro, no "in today's world", no history lesson before the answer.

### 3. Add something the top results don't have (information gain).
- *Why:* Google: "insightful analysis or interesting information that is beyond the obvious"; "substantial value when compared to other pages in search results" (people-first). "Don't just rehash what others already published" (Starter Guide). "Unique, non-commodity content" (Mueller, May 2025). Raters: "typical" and "average" pages = Medium, not High (QRG 7.1). Summaries of others with little added value = low originality / Lowest at scale (QRG 4.6.6, 5.2.1). Google patented a way to score "additional information … beyond" what a user already saw (US11354342B2; not confirmed in use).
- *How:* Read the top 5 results (and the follow-up questions around the topic, since AI Overviews pull from fan-out searches: only 38% of AIO citations are top-10 pages, Ahrefs 2026). List what they all say. Then add at least one thing they don't: your own data, a test, a real example, a local detail, a cost breakdown, a mistake you made, a clear opinion you can defend. If you can't add anything, don't publish the page.

### 4. Show first-hand experience, with proof.
- *Why:* The extra "E": "actual use of a product, having actually visited a place" (Google, Dec 2022). Raters trust the reviewer "who has personally used the product" (QRG 3.4). "Original photos or video" and "a personal perspective based on first-hand life experience" = high originality (QRG 7.1). Google reviews guidance: "Provide evidence such as visuals, audio, or other links of your own experience."
- *How:* Write what only someone who did it would know: what it cost, how long it took, what broke, what surprised you. Add your own photos or screenshots, test numbers and dates. "We tested 6 and returned 2" beats "the best options are".

### 5. Be specific: numbers, names, places, dates, units.
- *Why:* In the GEO paper, Statistics Addition raised visibility in AI answers by about 31% and pairs best with fluency (KDD 2024). Heavily cited ChatGPT text has 20.6% entity density vs ~5–8% in normal English (Indig/Gauge, correlational). Google reviews guidance: "Share quantitative measurements." Pages with "only commonly known facts" are rated Low (QRG 5.2).
- *How:* Replace adjectives with measurements ("fast" → "loads in 1.2 s on 4G"), generic nouns with names (the tool, model, city, street, standard), "recently" with a date. Every number has a source or your own method behind it. Never invent one to look citable.

### 6. Cite your sources, quote real people, and become a source yourself.
- *Why:* GEO paper: Cite Sources, Quotation Addition and Statistics Addition gave "30-40%" more visibility; Cite Sources gave the 5th-ranked page +115.1% (KDD 2024). Google's expertise questions ask for "clear sourcing"; very high quality news includes "a description of primary sources" (QRG 8.1). But "Attribution or giving credit to other sources doesn't replace the need for original effort" (people-first page, 2026).
- *How:* Link the primary source (the study, the docs, the filing) right where the claim is made. Quote experts or customers word for word, with their name and role. Then make sure the page has its own contribution (principle 3): citations support your point, they aren't the point.

### 7. Write statements that still make sense when quoted alone.
- *Why:* Cited ChatGPT passages are ~2x more likely to use definitive language, 36.2% vs 20.2%, and more often sit under question headings (Indig/Gauge, correlational). Fluency and Easy-to-Understand edits raised AI visibility 15–30% (GEO paper). Google's passage ranking system scores individual "sections or 'passages' of a web page" (ranking systems guide). Google: keep "important content … available in textual form"; "Break up long content into paragraphs and sections, and provide headings" (AI features doc; Starter Guide).
- *How:* Start each section with a plain sentence that answers that section's question ("A 301 redirect is…", "It costs $X because…"). Use headings people would actually ask. Put the facts in text, not only inside images or tabs nobody opens. Name the subject instead of "it" or "this" at the start of a section. Do this for the human skimmer; Google says not to make separate bite-sized "LLM versions" (Sullivan, Jan 2026).

### 8. Make who wrote it, how, and why obvious.
- *Why:* Trust is "most important" in E-E-A-T (people-first page; QRG 3.4). Google: "We strongly encourage adding accurate authorship information." Who/How/Why. Fake personas or AI headshots are "a form of deception" and Lowest (people-first page; QRG 4.5.3). Conflicts of interest lower trust (QRG 3.4).
- *How:* Real byline linked to a real bio (what this person has done in this topic). A short "how we made this" line (tested, interviewed, data source; and AI help, if a reader would wonder "How was this created?"). Disclose affiliate links or that you sell the thing. Never invent an author or credentials.

### 9. Get it right: fact-check everything, especially AI drafts and money/health/safety topics.
- *Why:* "Does the content have any easily-verified factual errors?" (people-first). Generative models "predict a likely sequence of words", so it's "critical to manually factcheck and review all AI-generated content" (gen AI guidance, 2026). YMYL content "must be highly accurate and consistent with established expert consensus" (people-first page; QRG 3.2). "Mild inaccuracies on informational pages are evidence of Low quality" (QRG 5.2).
- *How:* Check every number, name, price, date and quote against its source before publishing, titles and meta descriptions too. On YMYL topics, stay with expert consensus or say clearly that something is your experience, not advice.

### 10. Let the effort show: cut filler, curate, and build what text can't do.
- *Why:* Effort is "the extent to which human work went into creating the content or the systems powering it"; "large amounts of text without manual oversight or curation" = little to no effort (people-first page; QRG 3.2). Filler "can artificially inflate content" (QRG 5.2.2). Google asks if content looks "sloppy or hastily produced" (people-first). Main content includes "calculators, online tools" (people-first page).
- *How:* Delete paragraphs that any page on the topic could contain. Edit for clarity and errors. Where it helps the reader, add the thing a summary can't copy: a comparison table from your own data, a calculator, a map, an annotated screenshot.

### 11. Keep it current, honestly.
- *Why:* Google flags "changing the date of pages to make them seem fresh when the content has not substantially changed," and says mass adding/removing content to seem fresh won't help: "(No, it won't)" (people-first). The Starter Guide: content should be "up-to-date". Google's "query deserves freshness" systems favour fresh content where it's expected (ranking systems guide). AI assistants cite pages that are on average 25.7% newer than organic results, though AI Overviews didn't (Ahrefs 2025, correlational).
- *How:* Update when the facts change (prices, versions, laws, screenshots), say what changed and when, then change the date. Put dates on time-sensitive numbers ("as of Nov 2026"). Don't bump dates without real changes.

### 12. Write the page other people will want to quote (earned mentions beat link tricks).
- *Why:* Raters look at "what others say about the website or content creators" (QRG 3.4). In Ahrefs' 75K-brand study, branded web mentions correlate 0.664 with AI Overview visibility vs 0.218 for backlinks (2025, correlational). A 2025 preprint finds AI search has "a systematic and overwhelming bias towards Earned media" (Chen et al.). For local queries, AI answers lean heavily on the business's Google Business Profile and directories (BrightLocal 2026). Borrowing another site's authority is spam (site reputation abuse).
- *How:* Publish something quotable: original numbers, a clear framework, a tool, a definitive explainer. Then make it easy to cite: one clear headline stat, a date, a method, a named author. For local businesses, keep the facts on the page identical to the Google Business Profile and listings.
