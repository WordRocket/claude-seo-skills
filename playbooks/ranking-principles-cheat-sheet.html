Ranking principles: the cheat sheet

How Google and AI answers judge a page, in 12 lines. Pin it next to your editor, or paste it into Claude before it writes anything. From Google's own guidance (people-first content page, updated Oct 5, 2026; Search Quality Rater Guidelines, Sept 11, 2025) and the research on what AI answers quote. Checked November 2026.

#PrincipleDo this
1One reader, one problemWrite one line first: who searches, the words they type, what "done" looks like for them.
2Answer firstThe title promises it, the first 2–3 sentences deliver it. No warm-up intro.
3Add what the top 5 don't haveList what they all say, then add one thing none of them do: your numbers, a test, a real example, a cost breakdown.
4Show you've done itWhat it cost, how long it took, what broke. Your own photos, screenshots and dates.
5Be specificNumbers, names, places, dates, units. "Fast" → "loads in 1.2 s on 4G". Every number has a source.
6Cite real sourcesLink the primary source right where the claim is made. Quote real people by name.
7Lines that work quoted aloneStart each section with a plain sentence that answers its heading. Facts in text, not only in images.
8Show who wrote it and whyReal byline and bio. Say how you made it. Disclose that you sell the thing.
9Fact-check everythingEvery number, price, date and quote against its source, AI drafts most of all.
10Let the effort showDelete paragraphs any page could contain. Add a table, a calculator or an annotated screenshot.
11Keep it current, honestlyUpdate when facts change, say what changed, then change the date. Never bump a date alone.
12Be worth quotingOne clear stat, a date, a method, a named author. Earned mentions beat link tricks.

Habits Google says don't help

  • Writing to a word count. Google: there's "no magical word count target, minimum or maximum" (SEO Starter Guide).
  • Repeating the keyword. Keyword stuffing is against Google's spam policies. In the GEO study it scored below baseline in AI answers.
  • An FAQ section for the rich result. Since Aug 2023 FAQ rich results only show for well-known government and health sites.
  • Special files or schema for AI Overviews (llms.txt). Google's AI features doc: you don't need new machine-readable files, AI text files or markup.
  • New date, same page. Google lists it as a warning sign.

3 numbers worth knowing

  • +30–40% visibility in AI-generated answers from adding cited sources, quotes and statistics; keyword stuffing scored below baseline. (Aggarwal et al., GEO, KDD 2024, arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735)
  • 38% of AI Overview citations rank in Google's top 10 for the same search, so pages outside the top 10 get quoted too. (Ahrefs, Mar 2, 2026, ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-citations-top-10)
  • 44.2% of ChatGPT citations come from the first 30% of a page: answer first. (Kevin Indig / Gauge, Search Engine Journal, Feb 17, 2026; correlational)

The prompt

"Use ranking principles to write a page about [topic] for [reader]. First show me the reader card, what the top 5 results all say, and the one thing we'll add that they don't. Ask me for my own numbers or examples; don't invent any."

Audit instead: "Audit [URL] with ranking principles: score each principle 0–2 with evidence, then the 5 edits that matter most, in order."
