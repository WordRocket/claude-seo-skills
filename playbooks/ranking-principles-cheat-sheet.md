# Ranking principles: the cheat sheet

How Google and AI answers judge a page, in 12 lines. Pin it next to your editor, or paste it into Claude before it
writes anything. From Google's own guidance (people-first content page, updated Oct 5, 2026; Search Quality Rater
Guidelines, Sept 11, 2025) and the research on what AI answers quote. Checked November 2026.

| # | Principle | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One reader, one problem | Write one line first: who searches, the words they type, what "done" looks like for them. |
| 2 | Answer first | The title promises it, the first 2–3 sentences deliver it. No warm-up intro. |
| 3 | Add what the top 5 don't have | List what they all say, then add one thing none of them do: your numbers, a test, a real example, a cost breakdown. |
| 4 | Show you've done it | What it cost, how long it took, what broke. Your own photos, screenshots and dates. |
| 5 | Be specific | Numbers, names, places, dates, units. "Fast" → "loads in 1.2 s on 4G". Every number has a source. |
| 6 | Cite real sources | Link the primary source right where the claim is made. Quote real people by name. |
| 7 | Lines that work quoted alone | Start each section with a plain sentence that answers its heading. Facts in text, not only in images. |
| 8 | Show who wrote it and why | Real byline and bio. Say how you made it. Disclose that you sell the thing. |
| 9 | Fact-check everything | Every number, price, date and quote against its source, AI drafts most of all. |
| 10 | Let the effort show | Delete paragraphs any page could contain. Add a table, a calculator or an annotated screenshot. |
| 11 | Keep it current, honestly | Update when facts change, say what changed, then change the date. Never bump a date alone. |
| 12 | Be worth quoting | One clear stat, a date, a method, a named author. Earned mentions beat link tricks. |

## Habits Google says don't help

- **Writing to a word count.** Google: there's "no magical word count target, minimum or maximum" (SEO Starter Guide).
- **Repeating the keyword.** Keyword stuffing is against Google's spam policies. In the GEO study it scored below
  baseline in AI answers.
- **An FAQ section for the rich result.** Since Aug 2023 FAQ rich results only show for well-known government and
  health sites.
- **Special files or schema for AI Overviews (llms.txt).** Google's AI features doc: you don't need new machine-readable
  files, AI text files or markup.
- **New date, same page.** Google lists it as a warning sign.

## 3 numbers worth knowing

- **+30–40%** visibility in AI-generated answers from adding cited sources, quotes and statistics; keyword stuffing
  scored below baseline. (Aggarwal et al., GEO, KDD 2024, [arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735))
- **38%** of AI Overview citations rank in Google's top 10 for the same search, so pages outside the top 10 get quoted
  too. (Ahrefs, Mar 2, 2026, [ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-citations-top-10](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-citations-top-10/))
- **44.2%** of ChatGPT citations come from the first 30% of a page: answer first. (Kevin Indig / Gauge, Search Engine
  Journal, Feb 17, 2026; correlational)

## The prompt

"Use ranking principles to write a page about [topic] for [reader]. First show me the reader card, what the top 5
results all say, and the one thing we'll add that they don't. Ask me for my own numbers or examples; don't invent any."

Audit instead: "Audit [URL] with ranking principles: score each principle 0–2 with evidence, then the 5 edits that
matter most, in order."
